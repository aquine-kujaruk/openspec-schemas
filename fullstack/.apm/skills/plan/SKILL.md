---
name: plan
description: Plan a change. Turns an intention that would change the product's behaviour, its code, a standing decision or a procedure into a change whose plan and delta specs validation reports ready. Use when the person states such an intention.
---

# plan

The artifacts are the handoff: everything decided ends up in the plan or in a delta spec.

1. `pnpm exec factory doctor`. When it prints anything as `missing` or `drift`, follow the `setup` skill first. A `note` is for the person: relay it once and go on. A `suspect` waits for step 6.
2. Decide whether the intention changes anything: behaviour, code, a standing decision or a procedure. A question about the system changes nothing: answer it and stop here. An intention about a change that is already open belongs to the `feedback` skill.
3. Choose the schema. `pnpm exec factory schemas` lists the schemas this project has, when each one is used and the workstreams it builds through, and the products it declares, each with its folder and its schema. Pick the one whose use covers the intention. When the intention needs two of them, open one change per schema. In a project with products, open one change per product the intention touches, each made with that product's schema. When no schema covers the intention, open no change: tell the person which schemas the project has and stop.
4. Open the change: `pnpm exec factory openspec new change <name> --schema <schema>`, the name a short kebab-case phrase of what changes. Then add to its `openspec/changes/<name>/.openspec.yaml`, below what the tool wrote:
   - in a project with products, the product it builds: `x-product: <product>`;
   - when the intention opened more than one change, the others: `x-linked: [<change>, <change>]`, in every one of them, so that each lists all the others.
   Validation reports a change not ready while its product is missing or of another schema, or while a link does not go both ways.
5. Read what stands: the specs under `openspec/specs` the intention touches, and everything under `openspec/adr` and `openspec/runbooks`. In a project with products, a product's specs are under `openspec/specs/<product>/` and its decisions under `openspec/adr/<product>/`, and a delta spec of its change sits at `specs/<product>/<module>/<use-case>/spec.md`.
6. Investigate every runbook `pnpm exec factory doctor` printed as `suspect`: it runs a script, a binary or a file the project no longer has. Find when what it runs left and what replaced it (`git log -S '<name>'`, the history of `package.json` and of the file), and write it under Findings. Then:
   - When this intention left it stale or touches its topic, carry the procedure in this plan tagged `@modifies` or `@replaces` with what replaced it, or `@removes` when what it served is gone.
   - When the intention is unrelated and what you found is conclusive, open a change of its own with `spec-plan`, named after the runbook (`refresh-<slug>`), whose plan holds only that procedure so tagged, with `skip_specs: true` and no phase, and the person's approval as its open question; tell the person it is there to approve, verify and archive.
   - Otherwise open nothing for it and tell the person what you found. When what doctor flagged exists where doctor does not look, report it too as a proposal for the schema's repository.
   Never write `openspec/runbooks` yourself: a runbook changes only through a change, so that verify executes it.
7. Probe every access the change will use, so that once the person approves the plan the implementation needs nothing more from them: an API behind an adapter, the repository, what is deployed, the logs, the state of production. Skip an access that a linked change builds; it does not exist yet. For each one:
   - Reuse the access procedure `openspec/runbooks` holds for it, or write one in the plan: how to reach it, where each secret is kept, and, under `@keys`, the environment variables it reads.
   - Run the minimum that shows the implementation will work: the key is there and one authenticated read call answers. Read the secret from where it is kept and never print it. Call `local` and `sandbox` freely; call `prod` only after the person approves that one read, as an open question.
   - Run it under your real permissions. When you plan in an auto or bypass permission mode, do not check permissions unless the person asks.
   - When it answers, tag the procedure `@probed:<YYYY-MM-DD>`, and write what you saw under Findings, with its source and the date. A standing procedure is modified only when something changed.
   - When something is missing (a key, the way to authenticate, a permission, approval to call `prod`), list it as an open question that says exactly what the person adds and where. The person may decline it: then tag the procedure `@probed:declined`, say so under Decisions, and go on. The implementation will fail on that access, and that part is left undone if it is never configured.
8. When the schema has workstreams, read the skill of each one the change will touch, under the `implement` skill's `references/workstreams/<workstream>/SKILL.md`. Its Owns line says where that workstream's files live and how they are named: a seam is signed in those folders and with those names.
9. `pnpm exec factory openspec status --change <name> --json` lists the artifacts of the change's schema. For each one, `pnpm exec factory openspec instructions <artifact> --change <name> --json` gives its instruction and its template: write the artifact as that instruction says. Write the whole first proposal from your own decisions before asking the person anything. The plan's instruction says which decisions are the person's; those go under Open questions, each with the option you picked.
   Before requesting approval, follow the plan instruction’s probe and evidence criteria for each affected phase and spec.
10. When `pnpm exec factory schemas` names a spike workstream for the change's schema and the change touches what a user sees, spike its screens now, so that the person sees them before the plan is ready. A spike is a throwaway HTML page in the change; the plan writes no product code and no end-to-end test, so a product with no source yet is spiked like any other.
   - Write each spike under `openspec/changes/<name>/spikes/` as `references/spike.md` says.
   - List each screen under Screens with `@page` (the page the implementation will build), `@spike` (its spike) and its journeys in plain words. A screen is a visual decision: when a convention or a standing screen settles it, approve it yourself (`@approved:<today>`) from a spike with one variant and say so under Decisions; otherwise give its spike variants and ask the person, with the spike open, as an open question. Apply what they ask to change, show it again, and tag it `@approved:<date>` when they approve it, keeping only the variant they approved, or `@approved:declined` when they choose not to look.
   - The spike workstream builds those screens first in the implementation, from the approved spikes, and writes their end-to-end tests: its tasks open the first phase that touches the screens, unticked like every other task. The data the spikes paint is the data the in-memory adapters of the `e2e` environment are seeded with: say so in the tasks of the workstreams that write them.
11. For a change spiked in step 10, when the product has no procedure of purpose `setup` and topic `local-run` in `openspec/runbooks`, write one in the plan: the command that serves the product, its port, and the environment `e2e` whose adapters are the in-memory ones. Leave it unrun: the product may not run yet, and verify executes it.
12. `pnpm exec factory validate <name>` and fix every finding that is yours to fix.
13. Ask the person the open questions, the way you ask the person anything, the approval always last. The approval links the artifacts themselves, not a summary: `plan.md`, each delta spec and each spike, as Markdown links to their paths, or to their reading copies when `pnpm exec factory reading` names some, as the flow instruction says. When they answer, record each answered decision in its artifact, tagged `@by:person`, and remove its question in that same update. When they approve, record `approved_at: <today>` in the plan's front matter and remove the approval, unless another answer given with it picked an option other than the one the plan proposed: then update the artifacts and ask for the approval again. Validate again after every update.

The plan is done when `pnpm exec factory validate <name>` prints `ready`, which needs the person's approval. Then, in that same turn and without asking again, run `pnpm exec factory handoff <name>` and do what it prints: follow the `implement` skill in this session, or, when the project's runner took the change elsewhere, tell the person where and stop.
